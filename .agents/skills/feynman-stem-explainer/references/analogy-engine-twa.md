# Teaching-With-Analogies (TWA) & Structure-Mapping Engine
> **Theoretical Foundation: Dedre Gentner (Structure-Mapping Theory, 1983), Shawn Glynn (TWA Model, 1991, 1994), David Treagust & Allan Harrison (2000)**

---

## 1. Structure-Mapping Theory (Gentner, 1983)

An analogy is **not** a metaphor of surface similarity. 
- *Surface similarity:* "An atom is small and round like a pea." (Useless, misleading).
- *Structural mapping:* "An atom has a massive central sun (nucleus) and lightweight orbiting planets (electrons) held by an inverse-square force." (Useful, relational).

True understanding occurs when the brain maps the **system of relationships** from a familiar Base Domain to an unfamiliar Target Domain.

---

## 2. The 6-Step TWA Protocol (Glynn Model)

When constructing an explanation, follow this strict 6-step sequence:

```
Step 1: Introduce Target Concept (The STEM mystery to solve)
   ↓
Step 2: Cue Familiar Analog (A tangible everyday physical experience)
   ↓
Step 3: Identify Core Features of the Analog
   ↓
Step 4: Map Relationships (Analog Feature  <===>  Target Feature)
   ↓
Step 5: CRITICAL STEP: Identify Where the Analogy Breaks Down!
   ↓
Step 6: Synthesize & Draw First-Principles Conclusion
```

---

## 3. Step 5: The "Analogy Breakdown" Protocol (Feynman's Safeguard)

Every single analogy in science eventually breaks down. If the explainer does not explicitly declare the boundary of the analogy, the student **will over-extend it** and develop deep misconceptions.

### Classic Examples of Analogy Breakdown:

| STEM Concept | Everyday Analog | Where the Analogy Breaks Down |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Electric Current** | Water flowing through pipes | Water pipes leak if broken; open electrical circuits simply stop flowing. Water molecules have mass and momentum; electrons in AC drift back and forth at millimeters per hour while energy propagates at near light speed. |
| **Bohr Atom** | Planets orbiting the Sun | Planets do not emit synchrotron radiation and spiral into the sun; electrons are quantum probability waves (orbitals), not tiny billiard balls with definite trajectories. |
| **Cell Membrane** | Castle wall with gates | Castle walls are rigid stone; cell membranes are a dynamic, fluid lipid sea (Fluid Mosaic Model) where proteins float like icebergs. |
| **Enzyme & Substrate** | Lock and Key | A lock is rigid; enzyme and substrate flex, bend, and morph their shapes upon binding (Induced Fit Model). |
| **Gravitational Wells** | Bowling ball on a rubber sheet | The rubber sheet analogy uses *Earth's gravity* to explain gravity! It assumes gravity to explain curvature, which is circular reasoning. Spacetime curvature operates in 4D, curving time as well as space. |

### Rule of Execution:
Whenever a core analogy is introduced, **ALWAYS include an explicit "Де метафора ламається" (Where the metaphor breaks down)** section to preserve scientific rigor.
